# AgenticAI Curriculum — from *What the Structure Does Not Carry* (Georg Zangl, 2026)

> **Maintainer's note.** This is the internal reference for the curriculum; the
> public front page is `README.md`. Structure as published: twelve numbered
> folders, each course carrying its own syllabus (the former standalone syllabi
> folder has been retired). Syllabi list the book as **recommended, not
> required**, and every graded component is answerable from the provided
> materials. The self-contained instructor/facilitator guides and in-room
> documents are the canonical versions (the earlier duplicates and the
> `_Standalone` suffixes have been removed). Licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (`LICENSE`),
> with third-party attributions in `NOTICE.md`.
>
> **Folder 8 (Standards & Policy) is withheld from the public release** while its
> contributions are in private circulation with standards bodies (see that
> section). The folder is intentionally empty in the public bundle for now.

A complete curriculum derived from the book. Same spine at every level: the
custody law, the six properties (context, authority, accountability,
coordination, execution coherence, institutional memory), the four axioms, the
nine-test evaluation protocol, and the six custody rules. Depth, pace, and
assessment change per level. External figures live in one place only (folder 7);
the teaching materials themselves carry none.

## 7_Evidence_Base — external corroboration and teaching figures
- Evidence_Base_CCAF_2026.docx — the curriculum's only external figures, in one file.

Source: *The 2026 Global AI in Financial Services Report: Adoption, impact and risks* (Cambridge Centre
for Alternative Finance, University of Cambridge, April 2026) — 628 organisations across 151 jurisdictions,
with BIS, IMF, WEF, World Bank Group, IDB, CGAP and AMF in the consortium. Contents: the source and how
to cite it; five opening figures for slide two of every deck; the central corroboration (the report defines
*collective forgetting* as the loss of institutional memory and of the capability to execute processes
manually, and ranks it the third highest AI risk overall at 51% of respondents); a figure register mapped
to each property, axiom and test; an insertion map naming the exact week or session for every figure; and
instructor cautions on respondent cuts and self-reported maturity.

The syllabi name this file; no syllabus, slide, worksheet, or casebook carries a figure from it. When
a new edition of the survey is published, reissue this one file and advance its version — nothing else in
the bundle changes. Third-party figures: see `NOTICE.md`.

## 10_Financial_Services_Governance — NOT teaching: the framework inside a regulated financial institution
Five sector guidance documents for second-line risk, model risk, internal audit, operational resilience, and
the finance function. Premise: the sector has both the most developed governance apparatus and the deepest
agentic adoption, so it reaches the gap between them first. The apparatus already asks for five of the six
properties — of systems with a locus, which agentic deployments do not have.
- MZ-FIN-001 — Why this sector reaches the problem first (property-to-instrument map; three lines; where to start)
- MZ-FIN-002 — When the risk is not in the model (extending model risk management from component to crossing)
- MZ-FIN-003 — Dependency, concentration, and the cost of saying no (census and substitution vs resilience rules)
- MZ-FIN-004 — The close, the controls, and the record (the finance function and financial reporting chain)
- MZ-FIN-005 — What Your Analysts Were Carrying (article, ~3,300 words; .docx and .md) — prose, not guidance:
  the risk data aggregation failure as the way into the argument, for circulation or publication

Draws survey figures from folder 7 and (when published) the dated legislative status from folder 8. Not legal,
regulatory, audit, or accounting advice. See 10_Financial_Services_Governance/README.md.

## Style — read before regenerating anything
Palette "Stone": neutral greyscale, no hue anywhere. Georgia throughout — headings and body, docs and
decks. Ink 1B1A18 · heading 33302B · subhead 57534A · muted 6E6A62 · strong rule 8A8378 · rule D6D2C9 ·
tint C4BDB0 · shades F4F2EE / EBE7DF / E4DFD5. Body 10pt (Georgia reads a size larger than Calibri did);
deck type is pulled back ~6%, and letterspaced small-caps labels a further 10%, because Georgia is wider.

Deck two-state comparisons carry meaning by tone rather than hue: the recessive column ("without custody",
"what actually happened") takes 8A8378, the emphatic one 33302B. Each column keeps its own text heading, so
nothing depends on colour alone.

Do NOT reintroduce the retired navy-and-gold scheme (16224A, C79A3F, 1B2334, 22315F, 55617E, CAD9F4, EEF2FA,
B24A38, 3C6E57) or Calibri/Cambria. The curriculum is deliberately neutral: adopting institutions restyle it
into their own identity, and it carries no commercial mark of its own.

## Instructor guides — v2.0, self-contained
One self-contained instructor/facilitator guide per teaching offering, replacing the earlier practice of
pointing at chapter numbers. Each carries its own content: an instructor prepares a session from the guide
alone. These are the canonical guides (the earlier short "Instructors' Guide" / "Facilitators' Guide"
duplicates have been removed, and the `_Standalone` suffix dropped).

- 1_BSc_Course_Materials/BSc_Instructor_Guide.docx (MZ-EDU-IG-BSC, 14 weeks)
- 2_MSc_Course_Materials/MSc_Instructor_Guide.docx (MZ-EDU-IG-MSC, 13 weeks)
- 3_MBA_Course_Materials/MBA_Instructor_Guide.docx (MZ-EDU-IG-MBA, 12 sessions)
- 4_Professional_Certificate_Materials/PCert_Instructor_Guide.docx (MZ-EDU-IG-PCERT, 8 weeks)
- 5_Regulators_Seminar_Materials/Regulators_Seminar_Facilitator_Guide.docx (MZ-EDU-IG-REG, 6 modules)
- 6_Law_Course_Materials/Law_Course_Instructor_Guide.docx (MZ-EDU-IG-LAW, 13 weeks)
- 11_Financial_Services_Certificate/MZ-EDU-FS-003_Instructor_Guide.docx (MZ-EDU-IG-FS, 6 modules)

Structure, identical in all seven:
- **Part A — The argument in full.** The two observations and the law; the derivation of why exactly six
  properties, including the collapse of the usual candidates (security, privacy, auditability, integrity,
  compliance); all six properties with definition, custody requirement, characteristic failure and diagnostic
  test; the four axioms with their statements and the two eliminations; the ten-item protocol; the four
  buyer-side traps; the six custody rules and the rule above them. Shared verbatim across all seven guides —
  duplication is deliberate, because an instructor uses one guide, not seven.
- **Part B — Session notes.** Every week, session or module gets: Substance (what is being taught, restated
  inline), Sequence (the order that works), The question that does the work, and Errors to expect. Cross-
  references point to Part A section numbers.
- **Part C — The cases, with facts.** Full facts and sources for the cases that offering uses, so no case
  needs looking up. Each guide carries only its own subset.
- **Part D — Objections and recurring errors.** Level-specific, with the true part of each objection conceded
  before the answer.

Content source: all Part A and Part C material was taken from the manuscript verbatim or close-paraphrased,
not reconstructed from memory. If the book is revised, Part A and Part C are the only places to update.

## In-room materials — v2.0, self-contained
Three documents used cold, in the room, by someone who may not be the lead instructor and may not hold the
book. They carry their own content, on the same principle as the instructor guides, and are the canonical
versions (the earlier book-dependent editions have been removed).

- 1_BSc_Course_Materials/BSc_Tutorial_Worksheets.docx (MZ-EDU-WS-BSC, 14 tutorials) — the six diagnostic
  tests and the six characteristic failures printed at the front, with five supplied inputs.
- 2_MSc_Course_Materials/MSc_Derivation_Lab_Manual.docx (MZ-EDU-LAB-MSC, 13 labs) — written for demonstrators
  and TAs; carries the full derivation including the reverse challenge, the six tests, the ten-item protocol,
  and four supplied incident inputs.
- 4_Professional_Certificate_Materials/PCert_Applied_Exercise_Workbook.docx (MZ-EDU-WB-PCERT, 7 exercises) —
  includes "Northgate Orchestrate", a constructed vendor case with failures planted against eight of the ten
  protocol items, and facilitator notes mapping each.

**Provenance marking is mandatory in these three files and must survive any edit.** Each supplied input is
labelled either documented (traceable to a court judgment, regulatory proceeding, or official audit) or
constructed (invented for the classroom). Students must never see a constructed input presented as a real
incident, and must never cite one as evidence. The distinction between a sourced record and a plausible story
is part of what the curriculum teaches, and a teaching document that blurs it teaches the opposite.

## Attribution of drafting
Several documents carry a closing "Author's note" recording that the content is Georg Zangl's, drawn from the
book, and that the document was drafted with the assistance of Claude, with the author holding editorial
responsibility. It sits at the end, in small type, once — not in a disclaimer box at the top, where it would
read as anxiety and invite the reader to discount the substance.

The rule for where it appears: a document carries the note if it (a) enters a third party's official or
professional file, or (b) itself asks its readers to disclose or evidence provenance. By that rule it appears
in MZ-STD-001 and MZ-STD-005 (folder 8, withheld), and in MZ-LAW-002, MZ-LAW-003, MZ-LAW-004 (folder 9) and
MZ-FIN-004 (folder 10). Teaching material does not carry it.

## Syllabi — book-optional
The syllabi now list the book as recommended, not required. Weekly "reading" columns are labelled *optional
reading (book)* and point book owners to the matching chapters; the BSc "reading responses" were reframed as
lecture-based "reflection notes". Every graded component is answerable from the provided materials, so no
student is required to buy the book. This replaces the earlier policy of keeping required chapter reading in
the syllabi.
