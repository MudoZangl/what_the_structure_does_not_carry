# 10_Legal_Practice_Guidance — how to use the book's content for AI governance, by lawyer group

NOT teaching material and NOT a curriculum. Four short guidance documents (4 pp each) written for four
distinct categories of lawyer working on AI governance. Each explains how to put the book's content to work
in that role's own artefacts — policies, contracts, pleadings, practice notes — and each names the ways the
material can be used badly.

Distinct from the other legal folders: folder 7 teaches doctrine to law students; folder 6 trains regulators
and supervisors; folder 9 proposes the framework as normative standards content. Folder 10 is for practising
lawyers who already do this work and want the mechanism.

## The four documents

- **MZ-LAW-001 — for dedicated AI and technology counsel.** The governance-function role. Frames the six
  properties as the layer beneath whatever framework they already run (ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF, the AI
  Act) rather than as a rival to it. Core idea: a rule admitting no exception cannot be enforced by a
  probabilistic component, so every "never" in a policy needs a named enforcement point. Carries an honest
  treatment of the Article 14 human-oversight problem with three legitimate responses. Converts the nine
  tests into intake questionnaire, register columns, go-live gate, vendor review, and board reporting.
- **MZ-LAW-002 — for commercial and contracts counsel.** The leverage point: contracts bind suppliers where
  policy and rules do not. Twelve-row drafting schedule (concepts, not clause text) covering boundary,
  enforcement and authority warranties, evidence undertakings, independent verification, isolation,
  provenance, model change, consumption, dependency disclosure, substitution and exit, and a standing
  demonstration right. The drafting unlock is that the protocol needs no source code, weights, or training
  data — which is what survives a trade-secret objection where a conventional audit right does not.
- **MZ-LAW-003 — for disputes counsel and litigators.** The meaning-error pleading theory (data intact,
  significance lost at a boundary) as the alternative to pleading the model, which invites an expert contest
  on the opponent's ground. Record versus reconstruction and three questions that separate them. Disclosure
  requests ordered by how fast the material degrades — logs first, since retention may be months. Four
  questions for expert instruction, none needing model access. Horizon as the reference record, with a
  caution against over-reading it.
- **MZ-LAW-004 — for bar and law society technology committees.** Durability as the governing constraint on
  guidance that cannot be revised quickly. The nine tests rewritten as nine questions a lawyer with no
  technical background can put to a client's CIO — the most forwardable artefact in the folder. A section on
  the profession's own AI use mapped to the six properties, where the deeper exposure is advice that was
  competent, confidential, and unrecorded. Consultation responses as the committee's most underused
  instrument.

## Discipline for this folder

- Every document states plainly that it is written by an engineer, not a lawyer, states no law of any
  jurisdiction, and supplies mechanism for the reader to attach their own doctrine to.
- Every document declares the commercial interest, once, at the top.
- Every document tells the reader not to use the framework's vocabulary in their own work product. Custody,
  crossings, and seams have no legal standing; a pleading or practice note that borrows them looks borrowed.
  The concepts travel; the words should be the reader's own.
- Every document states the limits: custody of the six properties is necessary and not sufficient, and says
  nothing about accuracy, bias, dataset quality, or fundamental-rights impact.
- Each chapter map tells the reader what to SKIP. A chapter read without a use is a chapter forgotten, and
  guidance that recommends the whole book gets read by nobody.
- Article references and retention periods are given for orientation and flagged for verification. Live
  legislative status is held in 9_Standards_and_Policy/README.md — check there, not here.
- Style matches the bundle: Stone palette, Georgia, no colour. See the style note in the root README.
